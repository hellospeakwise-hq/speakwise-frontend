# Product

## Register

brand-product

## Users

**Primary — Speakers**: People building a public speaking career. They want a single credible record of their talks, topics, and audience feedback — not a LinkedIn post, not a scattered portfolio. Context: they're ambitious, builder-minded, and tired of having no portable reputation outside of Twitter clout. Job to be done: establish a verified track record that speaks for them.

**Secondary — Organizers**: Event organizers sourcing speakers for conferences and meetups. Context: they're time-constrained and rely on social proof. Job to be done: discover vetted speakers quickly, send requests, and manage the logistics in one place.

## Product Purpose

SpeakWise is the "GitHub for speakers" — a platform where a speaker's profile IS their reputation. It stores verified talks, structured anonymous feedback from attendees, and aggregated scores that compound over time. Success looks like: a speaker can send their SpeakWise profile link to an organizer and it carries more weight than a bio or follower count.

## Brand Personality

Credible, sharp, builder-focused.

Voice: direct, no fluff, confident. The kind of tool that takes your craft seriously because you do. Not cheerleader energy — earned-credibility energy.

Emotional goal: speakers should feel like they're building something durable, not posting into a void.

## Anti-references

- **Generic SaaS** (navy palette, gradient hero cards, hero-metric dashboards, "Trusted by 10,000+ users" social proof strips) — SpeakWise is a craft tool, not a sales funnel.
- **Eventbrite / ticketing platforms** — transactional, wrong impression. SpeakWise is about career building, not event registration.

## Design Principles

1. **Reputation is the product.** The speaker profile is not a form to fill out — it's a public artefact. Every UI decision should make it feel like something worth owning and sharing.
2. **Earned, not assumed.** Data and feedback are the credibility signal. Surface real numbers, real feedback, real history — not marketing copy or badges.
3. **Two registers, one voice.** The landing page and the app are co-primary. Dark, editorial, ambitious on the outside; clear, focused, precise on the inside. Neither should feel like a different product.
4. **Builder's tool, not a social network.** Minimal social noise. No vanity metrics, no follower counts. The data that matters is talk performance, audience reaction, and growth over time.
5. **Sharp without being cold.** Credibility doesn't mean clinical. Human faces, real feedback, community warmth — all within a confident, uncluttered visual system.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA. Reduced motion support required (Framer Motion animations must have `prefers-reduced-motion` fallbacks). Color contrast ≥ 4.5:1 for body text, ≥ 3:1 for large/bold text. Dark mode is first-class, not an afterthought.
